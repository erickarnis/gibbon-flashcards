# Gibbon — The narrative in five connections

A short framework for Chapters I–XXVI. These cards connect the detailed reigns; [the companion timeline and regional guide](ORIENTATION.md) provides a single-page reference. All dates are AD.

### 1. From crisis to reunion

<!-- ID: gibbon-00-crisis-recovery -->
<a id="gibbon-00-crisis-recovery"></a>

**Q:** Which turning points carry Rome from Alexander Severus’s murder to Aurelian’s reunification?

**A:** **235:** military revolt opens a prolonged succession crisis. **260:** Valerian’s capture and regional breakaways expose fragmentation. **272–274:** Aurelian recovers the eastern and Gallic territories.

### 2. From four rulers to one

<!-- ID: gibbon-00-tetrarchy-constantine -->
<a id="gibbon-00-tetrarchy-constantine"></a>

**Q:** How did Diocletian’s shared government give way to Constantine’s sole rule?

**A:** **293:** the Tetrarchy divides responsibility among two Augusti and two Caesars. **305:** the senior rulers abdicate. Rivalries and dynastic claims follow; **Constantine defeats Licinius in 324** and reunites the empire under one emperor.

**Sources:** [Gibbon, Ch. XIII, L1406](sources/mueller-volumes-1-2.md#L1406); [Gibbon, Ch. XIII, L1450](sources/mueller-volumes-1-2.md#L1450); [Gibbon, Ch. ### 3. The fourth-century succession

<!-- ID: gibbon-00-constantine-julian -->
<a id="gibbon-00-constantine-julian"></a>

**Q:** What main succession connects Constantine’s death to Julian’s sole rule?

**A:** **Constantine’s sons divide the empire in 337**; their conflicts eventually leave **Constantius II sole ruler in 353**. His cousin **Julian** becomes Caesar in **355**, is acclaimed Augustus in **360**, and rules alone after Constantius’s death in **361**.

**Sources:** [Gibbon, Ch. XVIII, L1894](sources/mueller-volumes-1-2.md#L1894); [Gibbon, Ch. XVIII, L1912](sources/mueller-volumes-1-2.md#L1912); [Gibbon, Ch. ### 4. From Persia to the Gothic settlement

<!-- ID: gibbon-00-julian-theodosius -->
<a id="gibbon-00-julian-theodosius"></a>

**Q:** What sequence connects Julian’s death in Persia to Theodosius’s eastern settlement?

**A:** **Julian dies in 363 → Jovian → Valentinian I and Valens in 364.** After **Valens falls at Adrianople in 378**, the western emperor **Gratian appoints Theodosius I in 379**; the Gothic war ends in settlement by **382**.

### 5. From persecuted church to imperial church

<!-- ID: gibbon-00-church-power -->
<a id="gibbon-00-church-power"></a>

**Q:** How did imperial favour change the church’s political position between Decius and Constantine’s successors?

**A:** A community pressed to sacrifice under **Decius in 250** became a recipient of imperial support under **Constantine**. Bishops could then seek state enforcement against Christian rivals; the emperor’s favour both strengthened the church and made doctrine vulnerable to court politics.

**Sources:** [Gibbon, Ch. XVI, L1735](sources/mueller-volumes-1-2.md#L1735); [Gibbon, Ch. XX, L2024](sources/mueller-volumes-1-2.md#L2024); [Gibbon, Ch. XXI, L2059](sources/mueller-volumes-1-2.md#L2059); [Gibbon, Ch. XXI, L2097](sources/mueller-volumes-1-2.md#L2097).
