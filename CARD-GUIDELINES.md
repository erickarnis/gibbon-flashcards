# Guidelines for Gibbon flashcards

## Purpose and scope

Help Eric remember the emperors, the succession of events, and Gibbon's narrative and themes. Mueller's abridgment is the starting point. The requested scope is now **Volumes I–II, Chapters I–XXVI**, through Theodosius's Gothic settlement. Preserve the reader’s settled historical decisions. The reader has authorised revision of the full collection, including the opening batch from Nerva through Marcus Aurelius. Memorable quotations are a priority alongside the historical narrative.

Keep the collection sustainable across many emperors. Use a small, variable number of cards per ruler; the current 4–8 is a working example, not a quota. Preserve distinctive people, episodes, and figures instead of reducing each emperor to accession and death dates.

## What to ask

- When and how the emperor ascended: predecessor, adoption, inheritance, military support, election, or usurpation as relevant.
- When and how the reign ended: death, deposition, abdication, and successor. Include a specific illness or mechanism where supported.
- Major achievements, policies, wars, and failures.
- Memorable anecdotes, striking figures, and distinctive associates.
- Connections between reigns and the larger argument: succession, military power, institutions, character, religion, and decline.

Ask one coherent question. Closely connected details can belong together: year, manner of death, and successor form a useful exit card. Avoid arbitrary lists or a complete biography on one card. Use years by default; add exact dates only when they matter to the story. Distinguish adoption, designation as heir, joint rule, and sole accession.

## Answers and context

Lead with a short, direct answer. Add detail only if it explains the event, makes it memorable, or helps distinguish it from similar events. Bold the essential names and dates sparingly.

Assume basic knowledge of Roman history. Each card must still stand alone in a shuffled deck: identify less familiar people by their relevant relationship or role, and explain any particular legend needed for the point to land.

- Write **Plotina, Trajan's widow**, rather than an unexplained name.
- For the Terminus joke, explain the boundary god's refusal to let his shrine be moved for Jupiter's temple in Rome's age of kings.
- Omit generic reminders such as explaining that the Praetorians were the emperor's guard.

Use **More** only for worthwhile historical colour: an additional event, relationship, figure, or story. Leave it absent when it adds nothing. Instructions about what to memorise, reasons for choosing a card, source-comparison commentary, and generic cautions belong in [editorial notes](sources/editorial-notes.md), not in study text.

## Literary style and quotations

Eric values Gibbon's literary style. Prefer his words where they make a good answer or add beauty, wit, irony, or a memorable image. Newly written prose can imitate his cadence, balanced clauses, and pointed contrasts while remaining clear and concise.

Aim for one worthwhile quotation per card when the text supplies one. Leave cards unquoted rather than force a weak passage. A quotation must earn its space beyond merely repeating the answer. Preserve enough of the sentence for its rhythm and meaning; avoid flat fragments.

When a quotation already answers the question, use it directly in **A** and omit the repeating paraphrase and separate **Gibbon** field. Add only the names, dates, or context needed to make it understandable. Background essential to an allusion belongs in the visible answer before the quotation, not behind **More**. Keep portrait-selection decisions out of captions; retain only useful identification or depiction qualifications.

Approved model:

> The veteran soldier contemplated the story of his own campaigns, and by an easy illusion of national vanity, the peaceful citizen associated himself to the honors of the triumph.

Rejected as uninteresting on its own:

> His mild disposition was respected by the good

Check quotations against the supplied edition. Mark internal omissions and distinguish direct quotation from newly composed prose. Exact wording is not a recall requirement. Keep these literary preferences specific to Gibbon unless Eric requests them for another subject.

## Historical accuracy

Integrate compatible detail from reliable modern references seamlessly. The answer should say that Nerva died after a stroke, rather than hide that detail behind a note about supplementary sources.

Keep source links with each card. Record provenance, discrepancies, and editorial reasoning separately. Attribute Gibbon's interpretations in the question or answer where necessary; avoid repetitive disclaimers.

For a substantial dispute, present the concrete issue and a proposed treatment to Eric, then wait for his decision on that issue while continuing unaffected work. Do not ask him to decide facts that can be checked. Preserve real uncertainty within a claim when the evidence cannot resolve it. Do not invent a diagnosis to make an answer more specific.

Previously settled disputes and chronology corrections are in [editorial notes](sources/editorial-notes.md); consult those before raising the same question again.

## Workflow

1. Read the relevant source passages and map the essential narrative, dates, and memorable details.
2. Draft in Markdown, with stable hidden IDs, one **Q** and **A** per card, optional **More** and **Gibbon**, and **Sources**.
3. Check chronology, source support, quotation fidelity, duplication, and whether each card makes sense alone. Preserve source passages so quotations can be checked later.
4. Review the readable cards with Eric. Apply feedback as a general rule where it clearly generalises, while preserving specific decisions already made.
5. Package for Anki only after content review and Eric's go-ahead. Creating or pushing a repository does not approve the cards or authorise packaging.
