# Source notes for the review draft

The source is the reader's Mueller abridgment, identified in `edition.json`. Saved passages retain that edition's actual text, including its errors and judgments. Line references are extraction anchors, not printed page numbers. Source comparisons and editorial rationale belong here; compatible historical details are integrated directly into the cards.

Opening-card numbers in the earlier notes below refer to the original 40-card draft. Current cards have stable link anchors; [the revision record](revision-2026-09-26.md) identifies mergers and subsequent changes.

## Reader decisions

1. **Hadrian's accession:** retain uncertainty in the answer. His accession in 117 is secure; the authenticity of the deathbed adoption is not. Gibbon, III, L718; [Herbert W. Benario, DIR: Hadrian](https://roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/hadrian.htm).
2. **Adoption and merit:** teach Gibbon's moral argument with its qualification. Pius's biological sons died before Hadrian adopted him; family connections still mattered. Gibbon, III, L722 and IV, L759; [Historia Augusta, Pius, editorial note 6](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Historia_Augusta/Antoninus_Pius%2A.html#note6); [Encyclopaedia Romana](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/encyclopaedia_romana/severans/severans.html).
3. **Avidius Cassius:** retain the clemency anecdote and correct the death. Gibbon's “voluntary death” is contradicted by the account of officers killing Cassius. [Dio 72 (71), 27–28](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/72%2A.html); [Oxford Classical Dictionary: Avidius Cassius](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/548510335).
4. **Review before packaging:** leave the cards in Markdown until the reader has reviewed the content. No package has been produced.
5. **Revision of 26 September:** remove editorial filler and instructions about what to memorise from the cards. Incorporate compatible modern details seamlessly, including Nerva's stroke. Keep only quotations with beauty, wit, or a memorable image; a quotation is not required on every card. Preserve material uncertainty in the historical claim itself, such as Hadrian's adoption, rather than surrounding it with generic cautions.
6. **Further review:** assume basic knowledge; remove the generic explanation of the Praetorian Guard. The reader approved the Trajan's Column quotation as a model and requested more literary, punchy quotations, ideally one per card without forcing them. Identify less familiar people and supply the particular background required by allusions: Plotina is Trajan's widow; the Terminus story concerns the royal-period construction of Jupiter's Capitoline temple, not an event in Hadrian's lifetime.

## Chronology

- Nerva's accession is **96**, despite the opening paragraph's bracketed **98–180** for a period that includes him. Chapter III and the book's own imperial table support 96.
- Trajan's adoption and designation are **97**; his accession as emperor is **98**. The bracket in III, L716 should not collapse them. [Herbert W. Benario, DIR: Trajan](https://roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/trajan.htm).
- Commodus became co-emperor in **177**, sole ruler in **180**. Use that checked chronology rather than infer dates from the age and interval in IV, L759. [Benario, DIR: Marcus Aurelius, Family](https://roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/marcaur.htm).
- Lucius Verus's joint reign, **161–169**, is included in Marcus's accession card and the sequence note. The group of five is not every emperor of the period.

## Deaths and anecdotes

The opening chapters do not supply a complete death narrative for every emperor. Compatible details from the scholarly *De Imperatoribus Romanis* biographies and ancient accounts are included directly. Nerva's death card now specifies a stroke followed by death several weeks later, as reported by David Wend. Pius's specifies fever, described in Historia Augusta 12.4–7. These are source-reported descriptions, not independent modern diagnoses. Marcus's exact disease is unknown, so his answer retains that uncertainty. No specific disease is invented where the checked sources supply only illness.

Pius's final watchword is a reported ancient anecdote, drawn from [Historia Augusta, Pius 12.6](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Historia_Augusta/Antoninus_Pius%2A.html#12) and repeated by Weigel. The Historia Augusta is uneven in reliability; the card does not pretend to reproduce a verified bedside transcript.

The draft also adds a few widely useful anchors outside Mueller's opening emphasis: Nerva's clash with the guard, Hadrian's Wall, and the Antonine plague. Their provenance is recorded here and in the source links, without supplementary labels in the study prose.

## Other distinctions retained in these notes

- Card 1: the Senate's role supplements the brief accession account in Chapter III.
- Card 7: 110 feet is Gibbon's stated height for Trajan's Column, not a newly verified measurement.
- Card 8: Gibbon's description of eastern provinces does not establish durable control of every conquered territory.
- Card 14: extensive travel is secure; the card avoids turning Gibbon's claim to have visited all provinces into a literal itinerary.
- Cards 15 and 21: consolidation and comparative peace do not mean an absence of warfare.
- Card 20: securing Hadrian's deification is one ancient explanation of the name Pius, not the sole explanation.
- Card 22: legal protection for enslaved people is attributed to Hadrian and the Antonines collectively, not to Pius alone.
- Card 23: food preceding Pius's fever is not evidence of poisoning.
- Card 27: eight winter campaigns is Gibbon's formulation, not an independently reconstructed campaign count.
- Card 28: the plague card does not assert an exact pathogen or mortality estimate, nor that it caused Marcus's death.
- Card 32: household images illustrate a reported reputation, not universal practice.
- Cards 34, 38, and 39: happiness, the threefold view of religion, and civic degeneration are Gibbon's framing. The questions already identify him as the author of these judgments; repeated disclaimers add nothing to recall.

## Literary treatment

The words in the **Gibbon** fields come from the supplied text. New connecting prose follows his balance and irony where that remains natural in a short answer. It is not labelled as quotation. Facts and dates should remain easy to find; literary colour must not obscure the recall target.

Gibbon's judgments about the happiest age, character, religion, and civic decline are explicitly his. They are not silently converted into historical consensus. Descriptions of comparative peace do not imply the absence of frontier warfare or repression.

The emperor cards prioritise a compact narrative over exhaustive biography. The extra notes preserve figures such as Trajan's 110-foot column, the 42 years of the two Antonines, and Marcus's eight winter campaigns as figures stated by Gibbon. They are not separate mandatory numerical tests.
