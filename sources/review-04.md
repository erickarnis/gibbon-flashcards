## Chronology and compatible additions

- Constantinople: distinguish the start after 324 from dedication in **330**, checked against [Hans A. Pohlsander, DIR: Constantine](https://roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/conniei.htm).
- The three sons become Augusti in **337**. The broad geographic division follows their established spheres and [DiMaio and Frakes, DIR: Constantius II](https://roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/constaii.htm). The anomalous assignment of the new capital to Constantine II in L1894 is not repeated. Precise changes in Thrace and the Balkan dioceses are not made into an artificial list.
- Constantius's responsibility for the family massacre remains **uncertain**, rather than silently endorsing every assertion of guilt in Gibbon. DIR explicitly says he may have been the driving force. The card does not teach Gibbon's alleged forged testament as fact.
- Julian's **355 Caesarship**, **360 acclamation as Augustus**, and **361 sole rule** remain separate. Strasbourg **357**, abridged away in Mueller's campaign summary, is supplied from [Walter E. Roberts, DIR: Julian](https://roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/julian.htm).
- Constantius died in **November 361**, as L2160 correctly states. L2162's later December bracket is not followed.
- Julian died in **363**. L2254's bracketed **263** is a transcription/edition error; the immediately following election of Jovian in 363 and the verified DIR account resolve it.
- Nicaea was **325** and Crispus's execution **326**. L2020 incorrectly places them in the same year; cards retain the distinct dates from the surrounding chapters.
- Valentinian's death is a **stroke in 375**, following [Walter E. Roberts, DIR: Valentinian I](https://www.roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/vali.htm), whose description is compatible with Gibbon's report of a burst blood vessel. No narrower medical diagnosis is supplied.
- Gratian's first elevation was **367**, not 375; **375** is the death of his father and the elevation of his half-brother. Checked against [Roberts, DIR: Gratian](https://roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/gratian.htm).
- Gothic entry is **376**. Rather than assert a disputed exact year for the initial revolt from Mueller's compressed transition, the card teaches the causal sequence. Roman exploitation and failures of provisioning are restored from [Noel Lenski, DIR: Valens](https://roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/valens.htm) and [Ammianus 31.4–5](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Ammian/31%2A.html#4). Fritigern's role and Gothic allies at Adrianople follow [Ammianus 31.13](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Ammian/31%2A.html#13).

## Uncertainty and interpretive boundaries

- The foundation procession is a **legend**; the Crispus/Fausta accusation and bath are **a disputed story**. No invented sexual motive or poison diagnosis is taught as secure.
- Gibbon's explanations of court degeneration, weakened frontier discipline, delayed baptism, Julian's mixed motives, and religious faction are identified as **his arguments**. The compilation of later administrative institutions is not represented as a complete system created overnight by Constantine.
- Constantine's conversion is not reduced to a single private psychological event. Baptism, public patronage, and profession are separated.
- Nicaea's meaning is taught without treating all opponents of consubstantiality as one perfectly uniform party. Gibbon's speculative derivation of all Trinitarian thought from Plato is not made into a factual genealogy.
- Julian's admiration for pagan worship does not make him an impartial secular rationalist. Conversely, his discrimination against Christians is not collapsed into the mass executions of earlier persecutions.
- The Temple project failed; competing reports of natural disturbances are not certified as a miracle or replaced by an invented mechanical explanation.
- Julian's last speech and philosophical death are taught as **Gibbon's literary scene**, not a verified stenographic transcript. His killer is not identified. The descriptions do not assert the much later dying cry about the Galilean.
- Jovian's cause of death remains uncertain. Valens's cottage death remains one reported account; his body was not securely recovered.
- Gibbon's broad praise of Valentinian's religious impartiality is qualified by the historical restrictions on Manichaeans and Donatists, documented in Roberts's biography. It does not become a claim of universal religious freedom.
- No card teaches Gibbon's direct identification of European Huns with the people of Chinese histories as established migration history. Immediate Hunnic pressure on the Goths supplies the necessary narrative connection.
- The Gothic settlement card teaches the broad exchange of land/support for service and **Gibbon's interpretation** of military dependence. It does not reproduce his precise reconstruction of autonomous jurisdictions, a standing 40,000-man force, or uniform treaty clauses as securely preserved facts. Gibbon himself notes the absence of the original treaty.
- The last three future emperors introduced within the volume—Gratian, Valentinian II, and Theodosius I—have accession anchors here; their deaths belong to the later volumes and are not previewed as a second set of biographies.

## Literary selection and checks

Priority went to Gibbon's images and balanced sentences: collected statues without recovered genius; invitations indistinguishable from commands; government as theatre; taxes descending on the poor; a monarch still reigning after death; Julian's defence of innocence; the repose of one man against that of millions; the endangered cattle; the deserted threshold at Nisibis; a bishopric worth conversion; the fish fearing a pike more than an army; and the refusal to dissolve humanity into expediency.

Every literary excerpt was matched verbatim against its cited supplied paragraph. The final pass expanded most initially selected fragments to complete sentences, retained complete dialogue where appropriate, and removed four weak quotations. Quotations that carry Gibbon's partisan judgments remain attached to explicitly interpretive questions or answers. The stories use the supplied edition's American spellings; new study prose uses ordinary modern spelling.
