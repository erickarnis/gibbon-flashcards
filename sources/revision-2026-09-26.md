# Full-deck revision — 26 September 2026

The reader authorised the changes in the [original review](../REVIEW-2026-09-26.md), asked that every card stand alone, and requested the most famous and interesting passages on cards. The revised Markdown deck contains **281 cards, 157 Gibbon quotation fields, and 32 linked literary highlights**. Thirteen cards were added and nine duplicates merged. No Anki package was made.

## Editorial outcome

The deck now connects reigns through five overview cards, explains mechanisms behind the third-century crisis and later military disasters, and ends by returning to Gibbon’s opening concern: public safety depends on a ruler’s character. The [timeline and regional guide](../ORIENTATION.md) provides orientation without adding another list to memorise.

All 277 original cards were accounted for. The original review’s five corrections, eleven strengthening proposals, nine mergers, and 63 polishing proposals were applied, either directly or through an adjacent new card. Gallienus’s hostile literary portrait is deliberately unchanged; its new companion supplies the historical counterweight. Keep decisions remain judgments of relevance and editorial quality, not a claim that every minor fact has been independently re-established.

## Accuracy and context

- **Protection of enslaved people:** replaced “release or transfer” with compelled sale to another owner. The remedy in [Justinian, Institutes 1.8.2](https://amesfoundation.law.harvard.edu/digital/CJCiv/JInst.pdf) is not emancipation. The settled collective attribution to Hadrian and the Antonines remains.
- **Julian’s agents:** made the task explicitly about Gibbon’s contrast between one ruler’s repose and millions’ insecurity. His sweeping quotation is preserved as his account, not used to teach total abolition. For the service’s reduction and survival, see [Ruth O’Hara, p. 142](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/id/eprint/4593/1/An%20Assessment%20of%20the%20Notitia%20Dignitatum%20as%20a%20Historical%20Source%20for%20the%20Late%20Roman%20Bureaucracy.pdf#page=144).
- **Constantine’s administration:** specified the removal of military command from praetorian prefects within a longer reform process, rather than attributing all civil–military separation to him. [DIR: Constantine](https://roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/conniei.htm); Gibbon L1444 and L1823.
- **Julian’s two models:** identified “Antoninus” as **Marcus Aurelius**, not Antoninus Pius. Mueller omits the immediately preceding discussion of Julian’s *Caesars*, where Gibbon names Marcus Antoninus and his victory in the contest. See [unabridged Chapter XXIV](https://oll-resources.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/oll3/store/titles/1371/Gibbon_0214-04_EBk_v6.0.pdf) and [Julian, The Caesars](https://www.attalus.org/translate/caesars.html). The card presents Gibbon’s interpretation of Julian’s ambitions.
- **Julian’s retreat:** added the burning of most of the fleet, with the twelve retained bridge vessels in More. This compounds the supply problem without becoming a single-cause explanation. [Ammianus 24.7–8](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Ammian/24%2A.html#7).
- **Gothic revolt and Adrianople:** added the concrete traffic in dogs for food in exchange for slaves, including children, and a separate card on the decision not to await Gratian. [Ammianus 31.4.11 and 31.12](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Ammian/31%2A.html#4).
- **Third-century recovery:** added Postumus’s western government and Gallienus’s mobile cavalry response. The latter is a narrow counterweight to a hostile portrait, not a claim that Gallienus invented cavalry or solved the crisis. [DIR: Postumus](https://www.roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/postumus.htm); [DIR: Valerian and Gallienus](https://roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/gallval.htm). Claudius’s victory at Naissus follows [DIR: Claudius II](https://roman-emperors.sites.luc.edu/claudgot.htm).
- **Decius:** added the pressure created by public sacrifice and certification in 250. The concrete surviving example is an Egyptian certificate; the card does not make Christian refusal the sole purpose of the empire-wide measure. [University of Michigan papyrus exhibit](https://apps.lib.umich.edu/files/collections/papyrus/exhibits/papyri_james/1_2.html).
- **Labarum:** named and displayed Chi-Rho, with [Eusebius, Life of Constantine I.31](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/25021.htm) supplementing the monogram lost in the ebook’s text extraction.

## Self-contained cards

The pass considered the question, answer, More, and quotation as a single card, without relying on section headings or neighbouring cards. Where identification is itself the recall target, it remains on the answer side. Basic Roman knowledge remains assumed.

Added or sharpened identifications include Plotina’s existing relationship to Trajan; Julia Mamaea as Alexander Severus’s mother; Alexander as Elagabalus’s cousin and adopted heir; Herennius Etruscus as Decius’s son and co-emperor; Carausius as the founder of the breakaway British regime; Narses as the Persian king; Tiridates as Armenia’s king; Theophilus as bishop of Antioch; Pliny as governor of Bithynia; Delphidius as prosecutor; Jerome as Christian writer; Phidias and Lysippus as sculptors; and Anatolius and Sallust’s roles in Julian’s death scene.

The cards distinguish Septimius Severus, Alexander Severus, and Severus II; Constantius I and II; the emperor Tacitus and the historian; Eusebius of Caesarea and the chamberlain Eusebius. Particular allusions retain their necessary context, especially Terminus and the competing imagined graves for Julian.

## Quotations and caveats

All **32 passages in [Literary highlights](../LITERARY-HIGHLIGHTS.md)** now appear in full on their linked cards, alongside the rest of the deck’s selected prose. The selection includes the happiest age, three spectators of religion, history’s crimes and follies, the dreary prison, education and disposition, the sword and sceptre, cabbages and power, the historian and theologian, Julian’s defence of innocence, enthusiasm and imposture, two graves for Julian, the fish of the Danube, and the limits of public safety.

Three broken date brackets were replaced by the complete source wording. The religion, civic-poison, and humanity-and-justice excerpts now contain their full thoughts. Weak or mismatched quotations were removed, including the cross’s punishment history on the labarum card and the leaves-and-flowers digression on the theological vocabulary card. Other extracts were shortened where their retained clause remains clear. The three marked joins combine compatible passages; all were read in context.

Source-reporting reminders and generic cautions were removed from study text. Material uncertainty remains in Hadrian’s adoption, Marcus’s illness, the palace deaths under Constantine, Aper’s accusation, and other claims where it changes the answer. Removed details remain available in the preserved passages and batch review notes: Carinus’s alleged killer, the uncertain source of Julian’s fatal spear, reported prodigies at Jerusalem, the claimed nomination of Claudius by Gallienus, and Gibbon’s warning about hostile accounts of Valens. No diagnosis or secure guilt was invented to replace uncertainty.

The scope remains **Chapters I–XXVI of the supplied abridgment**. Famous passages from later volumes—such as the retrospective about “immoderate greatness”—were not imported. “Most famous and interesting” is an editorial selection, not an objective exhaustive ranking.

## Verification

The checker verifies sequential numbering, one question and answer per card, unique IDs, exact duplicate questions, local source anchors, fidelity of every Gibbon field, balanced quotation brackets, all 32 highlights on their linked cards, and the validity of retired-ID mappings. A separate audit accounted for all 277 original IDs and checked 862 local links without errors. Regression checks confirmed that an unfinished quotation bracket and a highlight removed from its card both fail validation. All three marked joins were read in context; the checker reports them for editorial attention, with zero validation errors.

This is a content and prose revision in Markdown. No finished Anki layout or device rendering is claimed, and no new independent modern-history verification of every minor assertion was performed.
